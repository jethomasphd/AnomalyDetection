How detection works

Four independent statistical methods analyze each stock every trading day. Each method looks for a different type of anomaly. When multiple methods independently flag the same day, we assign higher severity. Colored circles on the price chart mark flagged dates — red for high confidence, blue for single-method flags.

The consensus score is a weighted average of all four method scores (Ensemble 30%, Matrix Profile 25%, EWMA 25%, Fourier 20%). A day is flagged as anomalous when two or more methods agree, or when the consensus score exceeds the 97.5th percentile of its historical distribution.

Technical Details

1. Fourier Transform — Frequency-Domain Structural Change

Every stock has a characteristic "rhythm" — how its price oscillates over time. The Fourier Transform decomposes a price series into its constituent frequency components, revealing whether the stock is cycling at 5-day, 20-day, or 60-day intervals. When the energy distribution across these frequencies shifts significantly from the historical baseline, it signals a structural regime change.

How it works:

  • Compute the full-history frequency spectrum as a baseline (top-5 frequency components by energy)
  • Slide a 60-day window across the series and compute the local spectrum at each point
  • Measure the symmetric KL divergence between the local and baseline spectra
  • Flag dates where divergence exceeds the sensitivity percentile threshold
D(local, baseline) = 0.5 × Σ [ local × log(local/baseline) + baseline × log(baseline/local) ]

What it catches: Transitions between mean-reverting and trending regimes, sudden changes in volatility structure, shifts from choppy trading to directional momentum. These are structural breaks that other point-estimate methods often miss.

2. Matrix Profile — Subsequence Novelty Detection

The Matrix Profile algorithm (powered by STUMPY) answers a simple question: for every recent window of price action, what is the most similar historical window? If even the best historical match is poor, the current pattern is genuinely novel — something the stock has never done before.

How it works:

  • Define a subsequence length (10 trading days by default)
  • For each 10-day window, compute its Euclidean distance to every other 10-day window in the series
  • Record the minimum distance (nearest-neighbor distance) — this is the Matrix Profile value
  • Normalize all distances to [0, 1] and flag values above the sensitivity threshold

What it catches: Unprecedented price patterns, breakouts into entirely new territory, earnings reactions that don't match any historical analog, and "first-time" moves that statistical methods calibrated on history may underweight.

3. Statistical Ensemble — Three Independent Tests

Rather than relying on any single statistical test, the ensemble combines three approaches with complementary strengths. Each component is normalized to [0, 1] and weighted:

  • Z-Score (40%): Rolling 60-day z-score of closing price. Measures how many standard deviations the current price is from its recent mean. Simple, interpretable, effective for sudden moves.
  • Seasonal Decomposition (30%): STL decomposition with a 5-day (weekly) period extracts trend, seasonal, and residual components. The magnitude of the residual — what's left after removing trend and seasonality — indicates unexplained behavior.
  • Isolation Forest (30%): A tree-based machine learning model that isolates outliers by randomly partitioning feature space. Operates on three features simultaneously (price level, daily return, 20-day volatility), catching multivariate anomalies invisible to univariate methods.
ensemble_score = 0.4 × zscore + 0.3 × seasonal_residual + 0.3 × isolation_forest

What it catches: Statistical outliers, unusual price moves after controlling for seasonality, and multivariate outliers where the combination of price level, return, and volatility is unusual even when each individually might not be.

4. EWMA Trend Analysis — Momentum Deviation

The Exponentially Weighted Moving Average gives more weight to recent prices, creating a responsive trend line. The system measures how far price has deviated from this trend and classifies the trajectory — not just the position, but the direction of change.

How it works:

  • Compute a 20-day EWMA (roughly one trading month) as the trend baseline
  • Calculate deviation as a percentage: (price - EWMA) / EWMA
  • Classify trajectory over a 5-day window using the slope of deviation: breakout (magnitude > 80% of historical range), accelerating (slope > 0.02), decelerating (slope < -0.02), or normal
  • Flag dates where normalized absolute deviation exceeds the sensitivity threshold

What it catches: Overextended rallies or selloffs, momentum reversals, and stocks drifting persistently away from their trend. This is often the most immediately actionable signal for business users because it directly answers "how far from normal is this stock right now?"

Consensus Scoring

The four method scores are combined into a single consensus score using a weighted average. A day is flagged as anomalous if (a) two or more individual methods flag it, OR (b) the consensus score exceeds the 97.5th percentile of the full distribution. This dual-trigger approach balances sensitivity with specificity — method agreement provides high confidence, while the percentile threshold catches cases where multiple methods are elevated but none individually cross their threshold.

consensus = 0.20 × fourier + 0.25 × matrix_profile + 0.30 × ensemble + 0.25 × ewma
