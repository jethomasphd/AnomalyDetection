Anomalies Detected
An anomaly is a trading day where 2+ independent detection methods agree that price behavior is statistically unusual. The four methods are: Fourier Transform (rhythm shift), Matrix Profile (novel pattern), Statistical Ensemble (z-score + seasonal + isolation forest), and EWMA Trend (momentum deviation). When the machines agree, pay attention.
Actionable Signals

Signal Performance
Trade rules (walk-forward, no peeking):
• A signal exists only after the close of its detection day; the trade fills at the next session's close. Never the same bar.
• Every BUY/LONG opens a $10k long; every SELL/SHORT opens a $10k short. Same-direction signals stack into independent positions.
• Exits, whichever comes first: BUY/SELL close when price touches the trend target frozen at detection time; an opposite-direction signal closes every open position of the other side; a time stop closes anything still open after the max hold.
• Transaction costs are charged per side. WATCH and REDUCE are informational only — no trade.
• The dashed line is the benchmark: buy-and-hold scaled to the strategy's average deployed capital.
Walk-forward vs Live: detection is causal (a past bar's verdict is exactly what a live run that evening would have produced), so simulated history and the live record follow identical rules — and are reported separately below so they are never conflated.
Realised P&L
The dollar total from trades that have actually closed (an opposite signal arrived and locked the price in). This is money the strategy has "banked" — it cannot change if the market moves tomorrow.
Unrealised P&L
The mark-to-market of positions still open today. It will move with tomorrow's prices until an opposite signal closes the trade. Shown separately from realised so the numbers aren't conflated — an open winner can still turn into a loser.
Sharpe Ratio
Think of it as: return per unit of risk. Two strategies making 10% a year aren't equal — the one with less volatility has a higher Sharpe.
Rule of thumb: < 0 = losing on average, 0–1 = mediocre, 1–2 = good, 2+ = excellent (rare).
Computed from the daily mark-to-market equity curve (realised + unrealised), annualised by √252. This is the honest time-series definition — it measures what the whole book was actually doing day by day, not just closed trades.
Max Drawdown
Think of it as: the worst peak-to-trough slide you'd have lived through. If the equity curve hit +$5,000 and then sagged to +$2,000 before recovering, max drawdown = $3,000 — even though the strategy ended up positive.
Smaller is better. The percent figure is drawdown / total capital deployed (trades × $10k), so "2%" means the book was 2 cents of every dollar committed underwater at its worst moment.
Average Holding Period
Mean number of trading bars between entry and the opposite signal that closed the trade.
A very short average holding period means signals are flipping fast on the same tickers (choppy regime). A long average holding period means the Signal's calls tend to ride until the next clear reversal.
Profit Factor
Total $ from winners ÷ total $ from losers.
1.0 = break-even (wins exactly cover losses). Above 1.0 = profitable; a 1.5 means every $1 lost is offset by $1.50 won. Below 1.0 = losing money in aggregate.
More robust than raw win rate: a 30% win rate can still be highly profitable if wins are much bigger than losses.
Hit Rate by Signal
Win rate broken out by signal type (BUY, SELL, LONG, SHORT).
Why it matters: the overall win rate can hide the fact that one signal type is carrying the strategy while another is bleeding. If BUY is 70% and SHORT is 30%, you'd want to know that before trusting the headline number.
Computed on closed trades only.
Fourier Transform
Decomposes price into frequency components using FFT. Detects when the stock's cyclical pattern shifts from its historical baseline via KL divergence of spectral energy distributions. Answers: "Has the stock's rhythm fundamentally changed?"
Matrix Profile
For every recent 10-day window, finds the best historical match. If even the best match is poor, the pattern is genuinely novel. Uses the STUMPY library. Answers: "Is this stock doing something it has never done?"
Statistical Ensemble
Combines three independent tests, all causal: Z-score (40%) standardizes today's return by the ticker's own trailing volatility; Seasonal decomposition (30%) isolates the residual after removing a one-sided trend and weekday pattern learned from past bars only; Isolation Forest ML (30%) scores multivariate outliers with a model refit walk-forward, never trained on the bars it scores. Answers: "Do independent statistical tests agree?"
EWMA Trend
Measures deviation from the 20-day exponentially weighted moving average, standardized by that deviation's own trailing volatility — so a 3-sigma stretch means the same thing for a sleepy T-bill fund as for NVIDIA. Trajectory classifies what the stretch is doing: extending (growing), reverting (snapping back), breakout (extreme), or stable. Answers: "Is the current momentum abnormal for this ticker?"

How Signals Are Derived

Four independent detection methods analyze each stock daily. When anomalies are detected, they're translated into actionable signals. No single method controls the outcome — consensus is the signal.

  • Buy / Sell — mean-reversion: price stretched beyond the sigma threshold from trend (in units of the ticker's own deviation volatility) while the stretch is contracting or stable
  • Long / Short — momentum: price stretched from trend while the stretch is still extending or at breakout extremes
  • Reduce — regime change: both Fourier and Matrix Profile flag structural shift
  • Watch — anomaly detected but no tradable directional setup

Confidence: Strong (3-4 methods agree), Moderate (2), Developing (fewer).

Materiality gate: a trade call additionally requires at least 2 methods in agreement AND the price to be a meaningful absolute distance from trend (default 1%) — statistically unusual but economically trivial moves (a 0.1% wiggle in a T-bill ETF) stay informational.

Technical Details

1. Fourier Transform — Frequency-Domain Structural Change

Decomposes price into frequency components. When energy distribution shifts from baseline:

D(local, baseline) = 0.5 * Σ [ local * log(local/baseline) + baseline * log(baseline/local) ]

2. Matrix Profile — Subsequence Novelty Detection

For every 10-day window, finds the best historical match. If poor → genuinely novel pattern.

3. Statistical Ensemble

ensemble = 0.4 * zscore + 0.3 * seasonal_residual + 0.3 * isolation_forest

4. EWMA Trend — Momentum Deviation

Classifies trajectory: breakout (>80% range), accelerating (slope >0.02), decelerating, normal.

Consensus

consensus = 0.20*fourier + 0.25*matrix_profile + 0.30*ensemble + 0.25*ewma

Flagged when 2+ methods agree OR consensus exceeds the 97.5th percentile.
