How Signals Are Derived

Four independent detection methods analyze each stock every trading day. When anomalies are detected, the system translates them into actionable trading signals based on two dimensions: how far the price has deviated from its trend, and which direction momentum is heading.

Mean-reversion signals (Buy/Sell) fire when a stock is deeply extended from its moving average and the momentum driving that extension is fading. These are contrarian signals.

Momentum signals (Long/Short) fire when a stock is breaking away from its trend with accelerating force. These are trend-following signals.

Confidence reflects how many detection methods independently agree: Strong (3-4 methods), Moderate (2 methods), or Developing (1 method with high score).

1. Fourier Transform — Frequency-Domain Structural Change

Every stock has a characteristic "rhythm". The Fourier Transform decomposes a price series into frequency components. When the energy distribution shifts significantly from the historical baseline, it signals a structural regime change.

D(local, baseline) = 0.5 × Σ [ local × log(local/baseline) + baseline × log(baseline/local) ]

2. Matrix Profile — Subsequence Novelty Detection

For every recent window of price action, what is the most similar historical window? If even the best match is poor, the current pattern is genuinely novel.

3. Statistical Ensemble — Three Independent Tests

ensemble = 0.4 × zscore + 0.3 × seasonal_residual + 0.3 × isolation_forest

4. EWMA Trend — Momentum Deviation

Trajectory classification: breakout (> 80% range), accelerating (slope > 0.02), decelerating (slope < -0.02), or normal.

Consensus Scoring

consensus = 0.20 × fourier + 0.25 × matrix_profile + 0.30 × ensemble + 0.25 × ewma

A day is flagged anomalous when 2+ methods agree OR the consensus score exceeds the 97.5th percentile.
