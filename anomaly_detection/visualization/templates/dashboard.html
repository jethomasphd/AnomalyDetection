How signals are derived

Four independent detection methods analyze each stock every trading day. When anomalies are detected, the system translates them into actionable trading signals based on two dimensions: how far the price has deviated from its trend, and which direction momentum is heading.

Mean-reversion signals (Buy/Sell) fire when a stock is deeply extended from its moving average and the momentum driving that extension is fading. These are contrarian signals — the idea is that extreme moves tend to snap back toward the trend.

Momentum signals (Long/Short) fire when a stock is breaking away from its trend with accelerating force. These are trend-following signals — the idea is that strong moves with increasing momentum tend to continue.

Confidence reflects how many detection methods independently agree: Strong (3-4 methods), Moderate (2 methods), or Developing (1 method with high score). Colored circles on the price chart mark flagged dates — colors match the signal type.

Technical Details

1. Fourier Transform — Frequency-Domain Structural Change

Every stock has a characteristic "rhythm" — how its price oscillates over time. The Fourier Transform decomposes a price series into its constituent frequency components, revealing whether the stock is cycling at 5-day, 20-day, or 60-day intervals. When the energy distribution across these frequencies shifts significantly from the historical baseline, it signals a structural regime change.

How it works:

  • Compute the full-history frequency spectrum as a baseline (top-5 frequency components by energy)
  • Slide a 60-day window across the series and compute the local spectrum at each point
  • Measure the symmetric KL divergence between the local and baseline spectra
  • Flag dates where divergence exceeds the sensitivity percentile threshold
D(local, baseline) = 0.5 × Σ [ local × log(local/baseline) + baseline × log(baseline/local) ]

What it catches: Transitions between mean-reverting and trending regimes, sudden changes in volatility structure, shifts from choppy trading to directional momentum.

2. Matrix Profile — Subsequence Novelty Detection

The Matrix Profile algorithm (powered by STUMPY) answers a simple question: for every recent window of price action, what is the most similar historical window? If even the best historical match is poor, the current pattern is genuinely novel.

How it works:

  • Define a subsequence length (10 trading days by default)
  • For each 10-day window, compute its Euclidean distance to every other 10-day window in the series
  • Record the minimum distance (nearest-neighbor distance) — this is the Matrix Profile value
  • Normalize all distances to [0, 1] and flag values above the sensitivity threshold

What it catches: Unprecedented price patterns, breakouts into entirely new territory, and earnings reactions that don't match any historical analog.

3. Statistical Ensemble — Three Independent Tests

Rather than relying on any single statistical test, the ensemble combines three approaches with complementary strengths:

  • Z-Score (40%): Rolling 60-day z-score of closing price. Measures how many standard deviations the current price is from its recent mean.
  • Seasonal Decomposition (30%): STL decomposition with a 5-day (weekly) period extracts trend, seasonal, and residual components. The magnitude of the residual indicates unexplained behavior.
  • Isolation Forest (30%): A tree-based ML model that isolates outliers by randomly partitioning feature space (price level, daily return, 20-day volatility).
ensemble_score = 0.4 × zscore + 0.3 × seasonal_residual + 0.3 × isolation_forest

4. EWMA Trend Analysis — Momentum Deviation

The Exponentially Weighted Moving Average gives more weight to recent prices, creating a responsive trend line. The system measures how far price has deviated from this trend and classifies the trajectory:

  • Compute a 20-day EWMA as the trend baseline
  • Calculate deviation percentage: (price − EWMA) / EWMA
  • Classify trajectory over a 5-day window: breakout (> 80% of historical range), accelerating (slope > 0.02), decelerating (slope < -0.02), or normal

Signal mapping: EWMA trajectory is the primary driver of signal direction. Decelerating momentum on extended stocks triggers mean-reversion signals (Buy/Sell). Accelerating momentum triggers trend-following signals (Long/Short).

Consensus Scoring & Signal Derivation

The four method scores combine into a weighted consensus score. A day is flagged anomalous when two or more methods agree, or the consensus score exceeds the 97.5th percentile. The signal type is then determined by deviation magnitude and trajectory direction.

consensus = 0.20 × fourier + 0.25 × matrix_profile + 0.30 × ensemble + 0.25 × ewma
