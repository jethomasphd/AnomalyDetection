How signals are derived

Four independent detection methods analyze each sending domain's click time series daily. When anomalies are detected, the system translates them into actionable deliverability signals based on two dimensions: how far clicks have deviated from their trend, and which direction the trajectory is heading.

DROP signals (Warm/Throttle/Pause) fire when click rates fall below the moving average. Drops are the deliverability emergency — every send during a reputation crisis is either medicine or poison. The trajectory determines how aggressively to reduce volume.

SPIKE signals (Audit/Quarantine/Lockdown) fire when click rates surge above trend. Unlike stocks, click spikes in email are almost never good news — they typically indicate bot contamination, security scanner prefetching, or list attacks.

Confidence reflects how many detection methods independently agree: Strong (3-4 methods), Moderate (2 methods), or Developing (1 method with high score).

Technical Details

1. Fourier Transform — Frequency-Domain Structural Change

Every sending domain has a characteristic engagement "rhythm" — how its clicks oscillate over time due to send cadence, day-of-week patterns, and seasonal effects. The Fourier Transform decomposes the click series into frequency components. When the energy distribution shifts significantly from the historical baseline, it signals a structural regime change in engagement.

How it works:

  • Compute the full-history frequency spectrum as a baseline (top-5 frequency components)
  • Slide a 30-day window across the series and compute the local spectrum at each point
  • Measure symmetric KL divergence between local and baseline spectra
  • Flag dates where divergence exceeds the sensitivity percentile threshold
D(local, baseline) = 0.5 × Σ [ local × log(local/baseline) + baseline × log(baseline/local) ]

What it catches: ISP policy changes, ESP platform issues, authentication failures that structurally alter engagement patterns.

2. Matrix Profile — Subsequence Novelty Detection

The Matrix Profile algorithm (powered by STUMPY) answers: for every recent 7-day window of click activity, what is the most similar historical window? If even the best match is poor, the current pattern is genuinely novel — something the domain has never done before.

How it works:

  • Define a subsequence length (7 days — one week of email data)
  • For each 7-day window, compute its Euclidean distance to every other 7-day window in history
  • Record the minimum distance (nearest-neighbor distance) — the Matrix Profile value
  • Normalize all distances to [0, 1] and flag values above the sensitivity threshold

What it catches: Bot deployments, list bombing attacks, sudden ISP filtering, unprecedented drops from blocklisting. This method is weighted 30% (up from 25% in the stock system) because novelty detection is critical for email — "never-before-seen click pattern" almost always means an external event.

3. Statistical Ensemble — Three Independent Tests

Combines three approaches with complementary strengths:

  • Z-Score (40%): Rolling 30-day z-score of click counts. Measures how many standard deviations current clicks are from the recent mean.
  • Seasonal Decomposition (30%): STL decomposition with a 7-day (weekly) period extracts trend, seasonal, and residual components. The residual magnitude indicates unexplained behavior.
  • Isolation Forest (30%): A tree-based ML model isolating outliers by partitioning feature space (click count, daily change, 20-day volatility).
ensemble_score = 0.4 × zscore + 0.3 × seasonal_residual + 0.3 × isolation_forest

4. EWMA Trend Analysis — Click Momentum

The Exponentially Weighted Moving Average creates a responsive trend line for click counts. The system measures deviation from trend and classifies the trajectory:

  • Compute a 20-day EWMA as the click baseline
  • Calculate deviation percentage: (clicks − EWMA) / EWMA
  • Classify trajectory over 5-day window: breakout (> 80% of historical range), accelerating (slope > 0.02), decelerating (slope < -0.02), or normal

Signal mapping: EWMA trajectory is the primary driver of signal direction. Drops are emergencies (Warm/Throttle/Pause). Spikes are contamination (Audit/Quarantine/Lockdown). Direction matters first, then trajectory determines severity.

Consensus Scoring & Signal Derivation

The four method scores combine into a weighted consensus score (retuned for email). A day is flagged anomalous when two or more methods agree, or the consensus score exceeds the 97.5th percentile. The signal type is then determined by deviation direction and trajectory.

consensus = 0.15 × fourier + 0.30 × matrix_profile + 0.30 × ensemble + 0.25 × ewma
Deliverability Diagnostic Stack

When a DROP signal fires, investigate in this order (each layer can cause drops in the layers above it):

Layer 5: CONTENT ← Spam trigger words, image ratio, link density Layer 4: ENGAGEMENT ← List fatigue, segment staleness, send frequency Layer 3: REPUTATION ← Complaint rates, spam trap hits, blocklist status Layer 2: AUTHENTICATION ← SPF, DKIM, DMARC alignment failures Layer 1: INFRASTRUCTURE ← IP warming state, DNS records, ESP platform issues

Rule of thumb: If the anomaly is isolated to one campaign → start at Layer 5. If it spans multiple campaigns on one ISP → start at Layer 3. If it spans all ISPs → start at Layer 1.
