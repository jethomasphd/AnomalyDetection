How This Works (No Jargon, We Promise) ▶

We run four independent tests on every domain's spam rate every day. Think of it like getting four different doctors' opinions. When they agree, you should listen. Here's what each test looks for, explained using the Cool Runnings chant:

🎶 "Feel the Rhythm" — Did the pattern change?

Every domain has a natural "rhythm" to its spam rate — it goes up a little, down a little, following patterns from send cadence and list quality. This test (Fourier Transform) watches that rhythm. When the rhythm changes in a way that's structurally different from what we've seen before, it fires.

What it catches: Slow, structural shifts in how recipients perceive your email. Something fundamental changed in your sending or your audience.

🎧 "Feel the Rhyme" — Is this something new?

This test (Matrix Profile) asks a simple question: "In all the history we have for this domain, has the spam rate ever done this before?" If the current week's pattern has no good match in history, it's genuinely novel — a new verse in the song.

What it catches: First-time events — sudden spikes from spam traps, new list sources going bad, infrastructure changes affecting reputation.

💪 "Get on Up" — Do three check-ups agree?

Three independent statistical tests each look at the spam rate from a different angle:

  • Z-Score: Is today's spam rate far from the recent average? (Like checking your temperature)
  • Seasonal Decomposition: After removing weekly patterns, is there unexplained behavior? (Like ruling out "normal" causes)
  • Isolation Forest: Does this combination of spam rate + change + volatility look like an outlier? (Like a full blood panel)

When all three agree, that's three independent confirmations. Time to pay attention.

🛳 "It's Bobsled Time" — Is the trend gaining speed?

This test (EWMA Trend Analysis) tracks the momentum of your spam rate. Is it accelerating upward? Decelerating? Breaking out? When the momentum locks in, it's bobsled time — the sled is moving and you need to steer.

Trajectory types:

  • Normal: Spam rate is within its usual range. Peace be the journey.
  • Accelerating: Spam rate is pulling away from its trend — momentum is building.
  • Decelerating: Spam rate is reverting back toward its trend — things are stabilizing.
  • Breakout: Extreme deviation — something big happened.

How Alerts Escalate

The signal level is determined by how many methods agree and how severe the anomaly is:

WATCH: "Peace Be the Journey" — 1 test sees something. We're watching. No action needed. HEADS UP: "Feel the Rhythm" — The pattern shifted. Keep an eye on this domain. ATTENTION:"Feel the Rhyme" — This is unprecedented. The domain needs attention. WARNING: "Get on Up" — Multiple tests agree. Time to investigate. URGENT: "It's Bobsled Time" — Clear momentum. Act now before it gets worse. CRITICAL: "Sanka, Ya Dead?" — I warned you before. You didn't act. Now it's worse.

Google's Spam Rate Thresholds (For Reference)

Google Postmaster considers these thresholds for email deliverability:

Below 0.10% — Healthy. You're in good shape. 0.10 - 0.30% — Within tolerance. Monitor it. 0.30 - 0.50% — Google is watching. You should be too. 0.50 - 1.00% — Problem territory. Take action. Above 1.00% — Serious. Deliverability at risk. Above 3.00% — Critical. Your emails may not reach inboxes.

Remember: This system doesn't just look at the absolute number — it detects changes in your spam rate behavior. A domain at 0.4% that's been stable for months is less concerning than a domain at 0.2% that just doubled from 0.1%.

What To Do When You Get an Alert ▶

When a domain gets flagged, investigate in this order. Start from the top and work down:

Step 1: CHECK YOUR LISTS ← Did you add new recipients recently? Bad list = bad spam rate. Step 2: CHECK YOUR CONTENT ← Did the email content change? Spam trigger words? Step 3: CHECK SEND FREQUENCY ← Are you sending more often? Too much email = more complaints. Step 4: CHECK AUTHENTICATION ← SPF, DKIM, DMARC — are they still passing? (This data is in the CSV) Step 5: CHECK INFRASTRUCTURE ← IP reputation, DNS records, ESP platform issues.

Rule of thumb: If only one domain is affected → probably a list or content issue (Steps 1-2). If multiple domains are affected → probably infrastructure (Step 5).
